# Setting up CodeBridge

The whole way, from download to your first request. The app walks you through
the same steps — this page is here so you can read ahead, or check a step
without switching windows.

**You need:** a Mac on macOS 14 or newer (Apple Silicon or Intel), a ChatGPT
plan that can create custom GPTs, and a free [ngrok](https://ngrok.com)
account. No Node.js, Homebrew, Xcode or Terminal — the app bundles what it uses.

---

## 1. Install the app

1. [Download the DMG](https://github.com/jobbsystemrapporter/codebridge/releases/download/v0.2.0-beta.4/CodeBridge-0.2.0-beta.4.dmg) and open it.
2. Drag **CodeBridge.app** to the Applications shortcut in the DMG window.
3. Open CodeBridge **from Applications or Launchpad** — not from the disk image
   window. An app run from the image stops working once the image is ejected.

CodeBridge is a standalone app and is not registered with Apple, so macOS
blocks the first launch. This is expected, and it happens once:

**macOS 15 and newer**

1. Try to open CodeBridge. macOS refuses and shows a warning.
2. Open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**.
3. Scroll down. Next to "CodeBridge.app was blocked", click **Open Anyway**.
4. Confirm with Touch ID or your password.

**macOS 14**

Right-click CodeBridge.app → **Open** → **Open**.

After this, CodeBridge opens normally every time.

## 2. Choose project folders

1. Click **Add folder**, or **Browse** to pick one.
2. Choose the folders ChatGPT should be able to work with.
3. Click **Continue**.

Only these folders are reachable. Sensitive locations — SSH keys, cloud
credentials, Keychains, Mail — are refused even if something asks for them.

## 3. Connect securely

1. Create a free account at [ngrok.com](https://ngrok.com) if you do not have one.
2. In the app, click **Open ngrok**, sign in, and copy your connection code (authtoken).
   Paste it into the app.
3. In the ngrok dashboard, open **Domains** and create your free reserved
   domain — something like `example.ngrok-free.app`. Paste it into the app's
   **Your reserved address** field.
4. Click **Connect securely**.

Step 3 matters more than it looks. Without a reserved domain, ngrok gives you a
different address every time CodeBridge restarts, and the custom GPT you are
about to create has the address baked into its Action schema — so it would stop
reaching your Mac after every restart until you pasted a new schema into
ChatGPT. One reserved domain is included free, and it is the same address
forever.

CodeBridge now has a private HTTPS address to your Mac. This is transport
only: it is not an AI model and not the OpenAI API. Your connection code is
stored in your Keychain, or in a private file under `~/.codebridge` if your
Mac has no login keychain.

## 4. Create the custom GPT

This is the only part that happens in ChatGPT's own interface. The app shows
each value to copy, one step at a time.

1. Click **Open GPT editor** in the app.
2. Paste the **Name**, **Description** and **Instructions** the app generated.
3. Scroll to **Features** and make sure **Code Interpreter & Data Analysis** is ticked.
4. Under **Actions**, click **Create new action**.
5. Back in CodeBridge, click **I opened the Action page**. The Schema step appears.
6. Copy the **Schema** from the app into ChatGPT's Schema field.
7. Next to **Authentication**, click the **gear icon**. The field says "None"
   until you do, and the options below only exist inside that box.
8. In the box: set **Authentication Type** to **API Key**, paste the
   **connection secret** from the app into the **API Key** field, set
   **Auth Type** to **Bearer**, and click **Save**.

> The connection secret is generated by CodeBridge and belongs to your
> installation alone. It is **not** an OpenAI API key. Never paste an OpenAI
> API key here, and never share the CodeBridge secret.

If your ChatGPT is set to another language, the labels differ — for example
Swedish shows *Funktioner*, *Åtgärder*, *Autentisering*, *API-nyckel* and
*Spara*. The order of the steps is the same.

## 5. Test the connection

1. On the Action page, scroll to **Available actions** and click **Test** on
   the `codebridgeHealth` row. That is the call that reaches your Mac.
2. Come back to CodeBridge and click **Check connection**.

You can also save the GPT and ask it *"Check CodeBridge connection"*.

The app says **Ready** only after a real authenticated call has arrived — not
before. If it stays on the previous step, the call did not get through; see
[Troubleshooting](#troubleshooting).

## 6. Start using it

Ask your GPT normally:

- *"Show me my projects"*
- *"Open this project and explain its structure"*
- *"Find the bug, fix it and run the tests"*
- *"Show me what changed in Git"*

CodeBridge keeps running in the background after you close the window, so the
GPT stays reachable. Commands outside the safe policy pause and ask for your
approval in the app before anything runs.

## Troubleshooting

**The app will not open.** Open it from Applications rather than the DMG, and
clear the one-time Gatekeeper block described in step 1.

**The GPT cannot reach CodeBridge.** Your Mac must be awake and online. Check
that the connection is still up in the app, and reconnect from step 3 if not.
If you set up without a reserved domain, your address changed on restart: add
the domain in the app, reconnect, and paste the fresh schema into the Action.

**The GPT answers but never runs anything.** Check that the Schema and the
Bearer secret are both saved in the Action settings, and that
**Code Interpreter & Data Analysis** is enabled.

**Is the service running?** Open <http://127.0.0.1:4317> in a browser. The
CodeBridge interface should appear.

## Removing CodeBridge

In the app, open the **Start over** menu:

- **Erase all data & start fresh** — wipes all local CodeBridge state and
  returns to first-run setup.
- **Uninstall CodeBridge** — wipes state, unloads the background service,
  removes the launch agent and moves the app to the Trash. Empty the Trash to
  finish.

Your project folders are never modified or deleted by either option.

## Security

- CodeBridge reaches only the folders you chose.
- Risky commands require your approval in the app, and an approval covers only
  the exact command you were shown.
- Files stay on your Mac unless you ask ChatGPT to work with them.
- The public address exposes only the authenticated action surface. The app's
  own interface and local API are not reachable from the internet.

The full boundary, including what CodeBridge does *not* protect against, is in
[SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md).
